Reflections

Reflective Journal

Throughout my co-op term I'm recording what I'm learning, the challenges I face, and how I'm growing as an engineer. Each entry is tagged with the competency it connects to most.

July 2026

Mid-Point Reflection

Retell. At the mid-point of this placement, my day-to-day work has shifted from the drafting-heavy tasks of May into a broader mix of design calculations, tender submissions, and on-site support at the PWH construction site. The first few weeks were spent almost entirely on monorail and BMU drawing revisions, but June introduced more responsibility: preparing O&M manuals with less direct oversight, coordinating directly with AECOM, and representing finished work at site visits and government submissions to the Buildings Department. I also took on a larger share of the quotation and material submission process, which meant more direct contact with suppliers and more accountability for the accuracy of numbers going out under the company's name.

Relate. This is not my first term at Jardine, and that history shaped how quickly I settled back in — I already knew my coworkers, the software conventions, and the informal norms of the office. Mayer and Salovey (1997) describe emotional intelligence as the ability to perceive, understand, and reflectively regulate emotion in a way that supports thought and action; returning to a familiar team let me manage that adjustment quickly, freeing attention for the more technical demands of June. My DISC profile, S/CD (Supportive primary, Cautious and Dominant secondary), also maps onto the pattern of work I've gravitated toward: the Supportive trait in how easily I resumed work with the same coworkers, and the Cautious trait in the amount of checking and revision built into my routine.

Reflect. Beyond the technical work, this placement has been a personal reflection point. Hong Kong itself feels different from the city I grew up in, and my own perspective has changed just as much — I'm returning as an engineering student viewing the same streets and industry through a technical lens rather than a resident's. Being trilingual and having grown up locally lets me move between the English-heavy documentation side of the office and the Cantonese-speaking site and vendor conversations without friction. Of my four learning objectives, three are progressing well; Microsoft Project needs the most attention going into the second half, and I want to push deliberately on Creativity & Innovation and Networking & Relationship Building — proposing alternatives rather than only executing given specifications, and building relationships beyond my two direct supervisors.

May 2026

Resuming at Jardine

Coming back to Jardine for a second term meant I wasn't starting from zero — I knew the team, the drawing conventions, and the office rhythm already. The first two weeks were mostly AutoCAD revisions and quotation work (monorail and BMU track drawings, then quotations for Buildings 8, 9, and 11 and the HKU TL3 project), which let me ease back in on familiar ground before the pace picked up in June.

Self-Management
June 2026

Taking on site coordination

A real shift this month was moving beyond the drawing board into direct site and vendor coordination — visiting AECOM in person, assisting at the PWH construction site, and checking that as-built conditions (like pipe routing) actually matched what was drawn. It pushed me to communicate technical detail to people outside my discipline more plainly than drafting alone ever required.
