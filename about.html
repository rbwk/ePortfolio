Background

I'm a second-year Mechatronics Engineering student at the Lassonde School of Engineering at York University. Mechatronics drew me in because it sits at the intersection of mechanical design, electronics, and software — the disciplines I most enjoy combining to solve real, physical problems.

I'm currently on a co-op work term at Jardine Engineering Corporation in Hong Kong, working as a Student Trainee under Mr. Fong (Senior Manager) and Mr. Roger (Senior Engineer). My day-to-day spans AutoCAD drawings for monorail and building maintenance unit (BMU) track systems, method statements, O&M manuals, quotations, and tender submissions, plus site coordination with AECOM and the PWH construction site. It's not glamorous work, but it's taught me that the parts nobody notices — clean documentation, correct tolerances, a drawing that reads the same way to everyone on site — are usually the parts that matter most.

I was born in Canada, grew up in Hong Kong, then came back to Canada for high school in the Greater Toronto Area before returning to Hong Kong for this placement — so this co-op feels a bit like round two. I'm trilingual in English, Cantonese, and Mandarin, which has made site coordination and cross-team communication here a lot smoother than I expected going in.

Interests

My interest in engineering traces back to my grandfather, who loved cars and passed that along early. That's shown up in a couple of personal projects I'm proud of: I rebuilt a VEX Robotics competition robot almost entirely from scratch, and more recently I self-taught 3D printing and CAD to design a fully functional sim racing shifter — Hall-effect sensors, interchangeable cam profiles, the works. That project taught me something I try to carry into my engineering work generally: the difference between something that "works" and something that feels right usually comes down to details most people will never consciously notice.

I've also coached robotics, which turned out to be the fastest way to find the holes in my own understanding — explaining a mechanism to someone building it for the first time is a much stricter test than being able to build it yourself.

Outside of that, I'm into cars, sim racing, and generally building and tinkering with things by hand.

What I Value

Reliability that doesn't need to be pointed out, documentation clear enough that someone else can pick it up without me in the room, and treating "good enough" as a starting point rather than a finish line.
